Tyre checks for lorries and vans

Tyres are the only part of the vehicle that touches the road. Many roadside stops start with a tyre.

Updated 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Home › Guides › Tyre checks for lorries and vans

What to look for

  • Cuts and bulges in the sidewall or tread. Any exposed cord means stop.
  • Tread depth. Minimum legal depths are set by law and differ by vehicle type. Check the current figures for your vehicles, and replace before the limit.
  • Pressure. A tyre that looks low usually is. Use a gauge at least weekly.
  • Uneven wear, which points to alignment or suspension problems.
  • Stones and objects stuck between twin tyres.
  • Valve caps and wheel nuts present and tight.

When to stop

If the tyre has structural damage, do not drive. Report it with a photo.

Record it

Tyre defects are the most common thing drivers report. Having them in one searchable history helps spot repeat problems on one vehicle. See defect reporting with photos.

This is general information. Follow the current legal and manufacturer limits.
