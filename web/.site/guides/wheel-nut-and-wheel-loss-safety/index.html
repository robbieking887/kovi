Wheel loss prevention: wheel nut checks

A wheel coming off a moving vehicle is a serious danger to everyone nearby. It is also largely preventable.

Updated 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Home › Guides › Wheel nut and wheel loss safety

Why it happens

  • Wheel nuts not tightened to the right torque after a wheel is refitted.
  • Nuts not re-tightened after the first short run.
  • Damaged studs or worn wheel faces.
  • Wrong or mixed-up fixings.
  • Corrosion hiding looseness.

What drivers can check daily

  • All wheel nuts present, with no gaps.
  • Rust trails or shiny streaks radiating from a nut, which can mean movement.
  • Wheel nut indicators still pointing in line, if fitted.
  • No strange noises, vibration or pulling in the drive.

What the workshop must do

  • Torque to the manufacturer's value with a calibrated wrench.
  • Re-check torque after a short distance following any wheel work.
  • Replace damaged studs and fixings, never reuse damaged ones.
  • Record who did the work and when.

Report with a photo

If a driver sees a rust trail or a missing nut, take a photo, report it and do not drive until it is checked. In Kovi, that report reaches the office straight away.
