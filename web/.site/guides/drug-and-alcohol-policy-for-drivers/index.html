Drug and alcohol policy for drivers

A written policy protects drivers, the public and the business. It should be simple and enforced.

Updated 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Home › Guides › Drug and alcohol policy

The law

Driving while over the legal limit for alcohol, or while impaired by drugs, is a criminal offence. The alcohol limit is lower in Scotland than in England and Wales. There are also specific limits for certain drugs, and it is an offence to drive impaired by any drug, including prescribed medicines. Check GOV.UK.

What a policy should cover

  • A clear rule, often zero tolerance, that drivers must not drive or start work after drinking or taking drugs.
  • The morning after: drinking late can leave alcohol in the system the next day.
  • Prescription and over the counter medicines that cause drowsiness: drivers must tell the employer, and the employer must handle the information carefully.
  • When testing may happen, such as after an incident or on reasonable suspicion, and how it works.
  • Support: where drivers can get confidential help without fear of automatic dismissal, if you choose to offer that.
  • Consequences for breaching it.

Do it properly

Brief all drivers, get a signed copy, apply the policy fairly and review it. Get advice from an HR or legal professional for the details.

General information, not legal advice or a substitute for official guidance. Check HSE, DVSA and GOV.UK for current requirements.
