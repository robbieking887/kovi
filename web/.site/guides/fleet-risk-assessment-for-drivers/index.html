Why bother

Employers must assess the risks to their staff and others from work activity, and driving for work is part of that. Employers with five or more employees need to record the significant findings. See our duty of care guide.

Five steps

  1. Spot the hazards: vehicle condition, fatigue, loading, reversing, weather, phones, rushed schedules.
  2. Decide who could be harmed: drivers, loaders, pedestrians, other road users.
  3. Set controls: daily checks, training, policies, rest breaks, route planning, maintenance.
  4. Record it and tell your drivers.
  5. Review after any incident, and at least once a year.

Examples of controls

  • Vehicle faults: daily checks and quick defect repair. See defect reporting.
  • Tiredness: realistic schedules and breaks. See fatigue.
  • Reversing: banksmen and cameras. See reversing safety.

Pull it together in a fleet safety policy.

General information, not legal advice or a substitute for official guidance. Check HSE, DVSA and GOV.UK for current requirements.

Questions

Do I need a written risk assessment?

Employers with five or more employees must record significant findings. Smaller firms still have to assess risk. HSE has templates and guidance.
