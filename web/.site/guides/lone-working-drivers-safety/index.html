The risks

A driver on their own may have a breakdown, a medical problem, an aggressive member of the public or a late drop in an unfamiliar place, with no one close by to help.

What good looks like

  • Risk assess lone work for the routes and jobs you run.
  • Check-ins: agree how and when drivers contact the office, especially at night.
  • Reliable vehicle: a working daily check lowers breakdown risk.
  • Working phone with a charger, used hands-free only while driving. See our phone policy guide.
  • Know what to do: breakdown steps, who to call, when to leave a site that feels unsafe. See breakdown safety.
  • Welfare: breaks, food, drink and a safe place to rest. See fatigue.

Put it in writing

Add lone working to your fleet safety policy and cover it at induction.

General information, not legal advice or a substitute for official guidance. Check HSE, DVSA and GOV.UK for current requirements.
