Why lorries and vans fail roadside checks

The causes are repetitive. Most are visible to a driver walking round the vehicle.

Updated 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Home › Guides › Why vehicles fail roadside checks

The usual suspects

  • Tyres: low tread, cuts, bulges, wrong or mismatched tyres, underinflation.
  • Lights and signals: blown bulbs, cracked lenses, trailer connections not working.
  • Brakes: worn components, air leaks, imbalance. A driver may feel or hear these.
  • Load security: poor restraint, damaged straps, overloaded axles.
  • Wheel fixings: missing or loose nuts, rust trails around studs.
  • Leaks: oil, fuel and coolant.
  • Body and guarding: missing spray suppression, damaged mudguards, underrun bars.
  • Windscreen and mirrors: cracks, missing mirrors or cameras.
  • Paperwork: missing or out of date documents and tachograph issues.

What to do about it

  1. Put these items on the daily check, and make drivers look at each one.
  2. Take photos of anything that is wrong. They are better than words.
  3. Fix defects before the vehicle leaves, not after.
  4. Use history to spot a vehicle that keeps producing the same defect.
