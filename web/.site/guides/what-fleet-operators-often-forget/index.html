Safety guide

12 things fleet operators often forget to do

None of these are exotic. They are the ordinary jobs that slip when everyone is busy, and they are what shows up after an incident.

Updated 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Home › Guides › What fleets often forget
  1. Reading the daily checks. A check nobody reads is just paperwork. Look at the Today screen every morning.
  2. Closing defects. Reported is not the same as fixed. Every defect needs an owner and a closing note.
  3. Telling the driver what happened. If drivers never hear back, they stop reporting.
  4. Dates. MOT, annual test, tax, PMI and brake test dates in one place with reminders.
  5. Driver licence checks. At hire and regularly after, with a record.
  6. Driver CPC dates for every driver, so nobody lapses by surprise.
  7. Trailers. They get less attention than the unit but fail just as often.
  8. Tachograph downloads on time and actually reviewed.
  9. Induction for new and agency drivers. Show them how the check and defect reporting work on day one.
  10. Load restraint kit. Straps, chains and sheets wear out. Inspect and replace them.
  11. Incident learning. Near misses are free lessons. Write them down.
  12. Records you can find. If it takes an afternoon to find last month's check, you do not really have records.

The pattern

Most of these are about follow-through, not knowledge. A simple routine and one place for the records fixes most of them. Our small fleet guide has a five-step start, and Kovi is built to make the daily part automatic.
