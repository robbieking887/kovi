Safety guide

Accident and incident reporting for fleets

The first hour after an incident decides how well you can deal with insurers, police and claims.

Updated 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Home › Guides › Accident and incident reporting

At the scene (driver)

  1. Stop safely and keep yourself and others out of danger.
  2. Call emergency services if anyone is hurt or there is danger.
  3. Do not admit fault. Exchange details as the law requires.
  4. Take photos: vehicles, damage, road, signs, weather, number plates.
  5. Note witnesses' details.
  6. Call the office as soon as it is safe.

What the office records

  • Driver, vehicle, date, time and place
  • What happened, in the driver's words
  • Photos and any dashcam footage
  • Injuries, damage and other parties
  • Police or insurer references
  • The last daily check for the vehicle, and any open defects

Why the check record matters

A time stamped, searchable check from that morning shows the vehicle was checked and what was found. It helps with insurers and, in serious cases, with showing you had a working system. See Kovi's history.

Learn from it

Review every incident and near miss. What would have prevented it? Change one thing each time.
