The DVSA guide to maintaining roadworthiness, explained

DVSA publishes a guide that sets out what it expects from goods vehicle operators. This is a short summary of the main parts.

Updated 1 November 2026 · 2 min read

Home › Guides › DVSA roadworthiness guide explained

The big idea

The operator is responsible for keeping vehicles roadworthy. DVSA expects a system, not just good intentions.

The main parts of the system

  1. Driver daily checks and defect reporting. Drivers check the vehicle before use and report defects in a way that is recorded.
  2. Planned preventive maintenance inspections. Regular inspections by competent people, at intervals suited to the vehicle and its work.
  3. Brake testing. Brake performance tested and recorded at suitable intervals.
  4. Defect rectification. Defects fixed by competent people and the fix recorded.
  5. Records. Kept for a set period, usually quoted as at least 15 months in the guide.
  6. Annual test preparation. Vehicles and trailers presented clean and ready.

Why drivers are central

Drivers see the vehicle every day. The guide expects a clear route for them to report defects and to be told what happened next. Nobody should drive a vehicle with a dangerous defect.
