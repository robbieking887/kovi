Why it matters

Lifting and carrying are a major cause of musculoskeletal injuries in delivery work. Employers have duties to avoid hazardous manual handling where reasonably practicable and to assess the risk where they cannot.

Before the lift

  • Look at the load: weight, shape, how awkward it is, where it is going.
  • Check the route is clear of steps, wet floors and obstacles.
  • Use a trolley, sack truck or tail lift where you can.
  • Ask for help with heavy or bulky items.

During the lift

  • Stand close to the load with feet apart.
  • Bend your knees, keep your back straight and hold the load close to your body.
  • Avoid twisting. Turn with your feet.
  • Do not lift above shoulder height if you can avoid it.

Loading the vehicle

Plan the load so heavy items go low and near the door, and the last drop is nearest the door. Read our load security guide.

For managers

  • Write a simple manual handling assessment for the jobs your drivers do.
  • Provide aids and train drivers to use them.
  • Record injuries and near misses so you can see patterns. See incident reporting.

General information, not legal advice or a substitute for official guidance. Check HSE, DVSA and GOV.UK for current requirements.

Questions

Is there a maximum weight a driver can lift?

There is no single legal weight limit. The law requires a risk assessment that looks at the load, the task, the person and the environment. HSE publishes guidance on this.
